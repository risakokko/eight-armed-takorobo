Designing Autonomous Behavioral Switching in Eight-Armed Octopus-like Soft Robot: Physical Reservoir Computing Approach

Risako Suzuki, Takehiro Hasegawa, Kazashi Nakano, Max Austin, Yasuo Kuniyoshi, and Kohei Nakajima
Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo
IROS 2026

Overview

Biological behavior emerges from the dynamic loop between brain, body, and environment. By applying Physical Reservoir Computing (PRC), this study demonstrates how a flexible body itself can act as a computational resource.

💡Key Takeaways:

  • Passive Arms as Computational Power: Adding 4 passive arms alongside 4 active arms enriches body dynamics, drastically boosting memory and information processing capabilities.
  • Autonomous Behavior Switching: Using a single output layer, the robot can exhibit out-of-training behaviors in response to manual perturbations, and switch among predesigned behaviors triggered either by external control signals or environmental changes.
